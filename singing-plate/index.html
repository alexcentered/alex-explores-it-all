The arithmetic behind this frequency

As a rotation

ω = 2π f

--

Anything that repeats can be drawn as a point going round a circle. One turn is 2π radians, so f turns a second is 2π f radians a second. That circle is why π is in every formula on this panel.

As light

f × 240

--

Keep doubling and the number becomes a colour. Visible light spans almost exactly one octave, so every note has one place in the spectrum and notes an octave apart share it. An analogy: sound and light are different kinds of wave. Turn on Colour to see it across the console.

As a radio wave

λ = c / f

--

--

As a tuned circuit

f = 1 / (2π √(L C))

--

A coil and a capacitor pass energy back and forth at one frequency, like a weight on a spring. Tesla's resonant transformer of 1891 is two such circuits tuned alike: energy crosses between them only when both ring at the same frequency.

Harmonics · whole-number multiples

Every note that is not a pure sine contains these. Each is one more wave fitted into the same length, which is also why a plate has a ladder of resonances and not just one.

Intervals · small whole-number ratios

--

The figure is drawn by two tones at once: one moves the dot side to side, the other up and down. A ratio of small whole numbers closes into a still shape. That closure is what the ear hears as two notes agreeing.

The Earth as a resonator

Radio waves are trapped between the ground and the upper atmosphere, and resonate when a whole number of them fits round the planet. Light circles the Earth 7.49 times a second, which is c / (2π R): π again, because the path is a circle. The textbook cavity rings at that figure times √(n(n+1)). The real one rings lower, because the upper atmosphere is a leaky wall. Tesla argued in 1899 that the Earth could be made to ring electrically; the resonance was worked out by Schumann in 1952 and measured in 1960.

    Beyond Earth

    No contact with a non-human intelligence has been verified, so no frequency is known to reach one. What does exist is below, in lists kept apart on purpose: what has been measured or sent, and what has been claimed.

    Where a frequency cannot be played, Simulate shifts it by whole octaves until it can. Doubling or halving a frequency gives the same note in another register, so what you hear is the true pitch in a range a speaker can reach.

      Sounds and tones in contact accounts · claims

        Sherman's exercise is recreated on the Line test page, as a fair test against chance.

        Why none of the measured frequencies can be played here: sound needs air and space has none, so anything sent between stars has to be radio or light. Those radio frequencies are roughly a hundred thousand times higher than a speaker can go; they need a radio transmitter and a licence, and the band around the hydrogen line is reserved for listening, so transmitting in it is prohibited. In nearly every account above, the beings are said to communicate by thought, which is why none of them comes with a frequency.

        Bands are published measurements, rounded; they vary between individuals and studies. A steady tone in someone's band is something they can sense, and a few will respond to it, but it is not their language: real calls carry rhythm, pulses and sweeps that one pure tone does not.

        Go gently. Keep it quiet around animals, whose hearing is often sharper than yours, and stop if one seems distressed. Playing calls to wild animals can stress them and disrupt breeding, and is restricted in many parks and for protected species. Frequencies below about 60 Hz need a real subwoofer or the rig's driver: small speakers cannot make them.

          Range safety

          1. Ears first. Earplugs or defenders on before the tone. Above 1 kHz it is shrill at any useful level, and near the top of the range you may not hear it while children and pets still do.
          2. Start low. Bring the amplifier up from zero each session. Raise volume only until the sand moves.
          3. Wired and clean. Cable from computer to amplifier, system EQ and sound enhancements off.
          4. Mind the low end. Long, loud tones under about 60 Hz can overheat the driver or bottom it out.
          5. Know the stop. Esc silences the output immediately from anywhere on this page.
          6. Contain the mess. Tray under the plate. Sand travels.
          7. Liquids stay shallow and away from the electrics. A few millimetres in a dish on the plate. Keep the driver, amplifier and cables dry, and expect splashes as the level rises.
