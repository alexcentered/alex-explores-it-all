    What this is

    The exercise

    In Above Black, Dan Sherman describes sitting at a console with headphones on, facing a screen of boxes with oscilloscope-style lines in them. A tone played in the headphones. He was told to hum the tone in his mind, and says that after several days he could make a wavy line go flat by thought. This page recreates that: a tone, a line, ten boxes.

    What moves the line here

    Chance. A computer has no way to sense a thought, so a few times a second this page draws a random bit: one way and the line gets a step flatter, the other way a step wavier. Each is a fair coin. The page then counts. If willing the line flat did anything, the count of flatter steps would sit above half by more than luck allows, and the record below would show it.

    You hear the same thing you see. Two tones play a hair apart, which makes the sound throb. As the line flattens the tones move together and the throb slows; when the line is flat they are one steady tone.

    What to expect

    Most sessions will land within chance. About one in twenty will look unusual by luck alone, in either direction, which is why a single good session proves nothing. Laboratories ran this kind of test for decades, most famously at Princeton from 1979 to 2007, and reported a tiny effect that independent groups, and later the same lab, could not reproduce. This page will not tell you what to believe. It will tell you what your own numbers are.

    Could a computer do the real thing?

    Not this one, not by itself. To make a line respond to you and not to chance, the computer needs a sensor. An EEG headband reads the brain's electrical rhythms through the scalp, and with one a line that settles as you settle is ordinary technology: it is called neurofeedback and it is sold for meditation. That would be your own brain activity moving the line, though, which is a different thing from thought reaching a machine unaided, or reaching anyone else. Nothing published shows that happening, and Sherman's account is the only source for his.

    The random bits come from this device's operating system. They are unpredictable in practice but are not drawn from a physical noise source, as laboratory tests use.
