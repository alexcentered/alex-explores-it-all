The ideas that recur

  1. Only whole numbers of waves fit

    Anything with edges, or that closes on itself, can only hold waves that fit it exactly. That is why a plate has particular notes and not others, and why each note draws its own pattern.

    A plateA sphereA torusA stone room

  2. Size sets the pitch

    Bigger is lower, by a rule that depends on what carries the wave. The knob above compares seven cases.

    The size knobPlate setupA drop of water

  3. Simple ratios sound simple

    Two notes whose frequencies are 2 to 1, 3 to 2 or 4 to 3 blend. The same ratios between lengths are the oldest rules of proportion in building. The arithmetic is shared; whether a builder meant it as music has to be shown case by case.

    IntervalsKing's ChamberParthenon

  4. Push in time and it builds

    Drive something at its own note and each push adds to the last. How far it builds, and how long it rings afterwards, is one number, Q. Every sand pattern is a resonance, and so is every tuned circuit.

    Resonance curveSweep for patterns

  5. Two that share become one system

    Let two matched resonators share a little of their field and they stop acting alone. Energy passes from one to the other and back, and their single note splits in two. This is the heart of a Tesla coil.

    Two tuned circuits

  6. Symmetry decides the pattern

    A square plate and a round one, driven at the same note, draw different figures, because the pattern must share the symmetry of the thing. For a sphere the possible symmetries are those of the five regular solids, which is why they turn up in crystals, viruses and drops.

    Square and round platesSolids and their waves

  7. A field has a shape, and sharp points concentrate it

    Magnetic field lines never start or end; they loop. Coils set at angles make a field that turns. And where a conductor comes to a point the electric field is strongest, which is why a spark advances from its tips and branches.

    Field linesRotating fieldA spark

  8. Some shapes repeat at every scale

    A fractal is made of smaller copies of itself, exactly or on average. Lightning, lungs and coastlines are rough fractals. Most patterns here are not: sand lines on a plate are smooth curves.

    Sierpinski tetrahedronMenger spongeA spark

  9. An octave is a doubling, anywhere

    Doubling a frequency is the same step for sound, radio and light. Raise a tone by about forty octaves and the number you reach is a frequency of visible light. That is a way of labelling notes with colours. It does not mean the sound and the light are the same thing: one is moving air, the other an electromagnetic wave.

What holds, and what does not

Holds

  • The nine ideas above. Each is standard physics or mathematics, and the unit tests check the app's numbers against known results.
  • The King's Chamber is a 3-4-5 room in whole cubits.
  • Neolithic stone chambers resonate near 110 Hz (measured, 1996).
  • A complete Stonehenge would have reverberated for about 0.6 s (scale model, 2020).
  • A spark grown by the field alone has a dimension near 1.7, as published.

Open

  • Whether any ancient builder tuned a room on purpose. A chamber that size resonates near a low voice whatever the intent.
  • Whether the King's Chamber's proportions were meant as music. No text says so.
  • How the heaviest stones were lifted, at every site here but the Parthenon.
  • The age of the Osireion, which a minority dispute.

Not supported

  • That a pyramid or temple was a power plant or energy device. No measurement supports it.
  • That particular frequencies heal, or that a chamber was tuned to one.
  • That a given frequency reaches non-human intelligences. The app lists such claims as claims, and gives no frequency for any of them.
  • Torus-shaped energy fields round the body, or free energy from a torus.
  • Plato's matching of solids to elements, as physics.

"Not supported" means no measurement was found, not that the question is closed. The Line test page exists because one such claim can be tested fairly at home.
